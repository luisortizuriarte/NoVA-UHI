About the Campaign

Citizen Science • July 16, 2026

The Summer 2026 Northern Virginia Urban Heat Island Mapping Campaign was a citizen-science initiative launched to identify hyper-local temperature disparities across Fairfax County, Arlington County, and the City of Alexandria.

Organizing Partnership

Conducted during one of the season's hottest stretches through a partnership between George Mason University’s Virginia Climate Center (VCC) and regional nonprofit Faith Alliance for Climate Solutions (FACS).

About the Measurements

More than 80 volunteers organized into over 30 teams, mounting portable thermal and humidity sensors to vehicle roofs. Teams drove 30 pre-selected routes across urban corridors, commercial strips, and residential neighborhoods during three coordinated intervals (morning, afternoon, and evening) to track how heat built up and lingered throughout the day.

The study specifically targeted neighborhoods with dense asphalt and low tree canopy coverage. Organizers emphasized identifying vulnerable communities, such as residents facing high energy burdens or outdoor workers at heightened risk of heat-related illness.

What You Are Seeing Here

Researchers at the Virginia Climate Center and Virginia State Climate Office processed the mobile measurements through machine learning combined with satellite imagery to produce the continuous 100-meter surface maps shown here.

The maps show a spatial snapshot of temperatures on July 16, 2026, highlighting the region's hot spots, cooling tree canopy refuges, and their relationship to surrounding built environments.

Virginia State Climate Office

The Virginia State Climate Office (VaSCO) serves to develop and deliver vital and timely weather and climate information to all Virginians. VaSCO is part of the Virginia Climate Center at George Mason University.

Faith Alliance for Climate Solutions

Faith Alliance for Climate Solutions (FACS) unites people of faith, and friends who share our values, to develop local solutions to the climate crisis. Faithful voices are valuable in the conversation on climate change, and faith-based action draws upon our deep well of love for each other and all living things. Their work is done in Northern Virginia and nearby jurisdictions.
